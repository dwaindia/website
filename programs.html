Priority 01 • Women's Enterprise & Vocational Tailoring Cohort
Priority Pillar 01 • Economic Self-Reliance

Women's Empowerment, Micro-Enterprise & Livelihoods

Rural Dalit women and single mothers face acute economic marginalization, frequently subjected to bonded seasonal farm labor and usurious moneylenders charging over 60% annualized interest. DWA breaks this debt cycle by transferring tangible, income-generating productive assets directly into the hands of women.

Tailoring & Garment Production

Transfer of commercial-grade motorized sewing machines, cutting tables, initial cloth inventory, and 30-day professional master cutter instruction.

Subsidized Livestock Units

Breeding pairs of sheep and dairy goats with mandatory veterinary immunization, subsidized feed concentrates, and mortality insurance coverage.

Rural Micro-Retail Kiosks

Seed inventory, digital weighing scales, and financial ledger training enabling women to operate independent village provision stores and tea kiosks.

Priority 02 • Primary Scholastic Retention Distribution
Priority Pillar 02 • Educational Advancement

Child Education, School Retention & Learning Support

In rural agrarian hamlets, extreme poverty frequently forces first-generation learners out of school into seasonal cotton picking and casual farm labour. DWA eliminates the material hurdles to schooling through direct educational kits, evening study infrastructure, and community retention monitoring.

Annual Scholastic Retention Kits

Distribution of reinforced waterproof backpacks, comprehensive notebook sets, geometry instruments, writing supplies, and uniform subsidies.

Solar Study Lanterns

Equipping un-electrified and erratic-power Dalit households with rechargeable LED solar lanterns, enabling daily homework and night study.

Parent-Teacher Dropout Watch

Village-level retention committees that track student attendance weekly, conducting immediate home visits whenever attendance wavers.

Priority 03 • Borewell Filtration & Aquifer Recharge
Priority Pillar 03 • Ecological Resilience

Water Stewardship, Deep Aquifer Recharge & Drought Resilience

Kurnool and Nandyal districts suffer from severe semi-arid climate shocks, erratic monsoons, and high natural fluoride contamination in groundwater. Dalit colonies, often situated on village peripheries, suffer disproportionate water deprivation. DWA constructs lasting drinking water security.

Deep Borewell Installations & Filtration

Drilling deep potable borewells equipped with heavy-duty submersible pumps and multi-stage slow-sand filters to eliminate turbidity and sediments.

Gravity-Fed Surface Distribution

Laying PVC pipelines connecting central overhead storage tanks directly to Dalit residential colonies, removing the multi-kilometer head-load burden.

Monsoon Runoff Aquifer Recharge Pits

Constructing gravel-bedded infiltration channels that direct seasonal rainwater into subterranean aquifers, sustaining year-round groundwater tables.

Priority 04 • Community Mobilization & Preventative Health
Priority Pillar 04 • Grassroots Welfare

Community Welfare, Preventive Health & Civic Empowerment

Sustainable transformation demands resilient community institutions. DWA convenes village Self-Help Groups, delivers periodic maternal and preventative health camps, and acts as an immediate humanitarian first responder during regional climate disasters or economic crises.

Preventative Health & Hygiene Outreach

Quarterly village health camps screening for anaemia, hypertension, maternal health, and seasonal water-borne diseases with certified rural physicians.

Emergency Humanitarian Relief

Rapid deployment of dry food rations, sanitizing supplies, and direct relief survival aid to daily-wage households during floods, droughts, or lockdowns.

Self-Help Group Mobilization

Formalizing grassroots micro-finance passbooks, enabling digital UPI bank linkage, and training community leaders in government welfare scheme enrollment.

Privacy Policy

Institutional Notice: The following privacy terms reflect DWA's standard public data governance policy and remain subject to final written legal review by the managing committee.

1. Information We Collect: Dalit Welfare Association collects contact details (such as name, email address, phone number, and postal address) voluntarily submitted by visitors through enquiry forms, voluntary donation portals, and newsletter requests.

2. Lawful Basis and Purpose: All collected data is processed strictly for the administrative fulfillment of your donations, statutory FCRA / 80G tax receipt issuance, voluntary participation, or institutional correspondence.

3. Non-Disclosure & Security: DWA does not sell, trade, rent, or lease personal information to third parties or marketing agencies. Personal records are retained in compliance with statutory Indian accounting regulations and accessible only to authorized administrative staff.

4. Your Rights: Donors and correspondents may at any time request an update, review, or deletion of their contact preferences by writing to dalitwelfareassociation1@gmail.com.

Refund and Cancellation Policy

Institutional Notice: Official charitable donation terms governing domestic and international philanthropic contributions.

1. Voluntary Contributions: Donations made to Dalit Welfare Association are voluntary charitable contributions directly utilized to advance non-commercial educational, livelihood, and community development programmes in Andhra Pradesh.

2. Refund Eligibility: Once processed and a statutory tax receipt (under Section 80G or FCRA) has been issued, donations are generally non-refundable. However, if a duplicate transaction or clerical error occurs during electronic transfer, a refund request may be submitted within 7 calendar days of the transaction.

3. Review Process: Refund claims must be submitted in writing to dalitwelfareassociation1@gmail.com accompanied by proof of deduction, bank transaction reference, and original contact details. Approved refunds are credited back to the original source account within 14 business days.

4. Statutory Regulatory Compliance: Foreign contributions routed through designated State Bank of India FCRA accounts are subject to Ministry of Home Affairs statutory scrutiny and cannot be refunded to domestic Indian bank accounts.
