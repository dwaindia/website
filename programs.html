Priority 01 • Sirivella & Rudravaram

Women’s Economic Empowerment & Micro-Enterprise

Women in rural Dalit and female-headed households—particularly widows, single mothers, and women from Below Poverty Line (BPL) families—bear a disproportionate share of agricultural unemployment and climate shocks. DWA’s 12-month enterprise model transforms vulnerable participants into self-reliant micro-entrepreneurs by pairing productive assets with practical trade coaching and Self-Help Group (SHG) peer savings.

The source architecture outlines a 50-woman cohort model (aged 25–50) spanning value-chain scoping, transparent community selection, 3-phase capacity building, direct asset deployment, and ongoing mentorship. Specific participant allocations and asset packages vary by approved donor grant.

Tailoring & Textile Skills

Practical training in garment construction, stitching, and finishing, supported in documented cohorts with sewing machines, starter fabric supplies, and local customer development.

Sheep Rearing & Livestock Livelihoods

Climate-resilient livestock support using hardy Nellore/Deccani sheep breeds alongside husbandry training, feeding practices, and veterinary care or insurance where budgeted.

Rural Micro-Retail Enterprises

Support for small village grocery shops covering initial inventory setup, stock control, supplier linkages, fair pricing, and simple profit bookkeeping.

Historical Dairy & Vocational Trades

Builds on DWA’s historical Dairy Development Project in Sirvel and Govindapalle (supported by Global Compassion Inc. in FY 2016–17) as well as soft toy making and rural brick-making trades.

02 • Banking, SHGs & Market Linkages

Financial Inclusion, Digital Skills & Business Formalization

Sustainable rural enterprises require safe banking habits, separation of household and business finances, digital payment fluency, and Self-Help Group (SHG) revolving credit.

Financial Education & Rural Banking

  • Formal Bank Access: Guidance on opening and independently operating formal bank accounts and debit cards where included in project scope.
  • Budgeting & Bookkeeping: Practical tracking of household expenses separately from small-business working capital.
  • SHG Thrift & Revolving Credit: Organizing peer savings groups so members can access internal credit for feed, stock, or veterinary care without exploitative lenders.
  • Responsible Credit Awareness: Training on interest rates, repayment schedules, and formal institutional credit linkages.

Digital Inclusion & Market Linkages

  • UPI & QR-Code Payments: Hands-on coaching in mobile banking apps, QR payment acceptance, and reconciling digital transactions.
  • Business Formalization: Assistance with local trade registration processes, product presentation, packaging, and pricing clarity.
  • Market Connections: Linking rural women producers with reliable wholesale suppliers, local consumers, and village trade events.
Verification Standard: Source programme targets include at least 50% active digital-payment usage and 60% trade formalization. These represent planned programme targets, not confirmed endline outcomes, pending cohort completion reports.

Priority 02 • 10 Government Primary Schools

Child Education, Retention & Protection

During agricultural droughts, seasonal labour migration frequently forces children out of school. DWA works across 10 Government Primary Schools in Sirivella and Bandi Atmakur Mandals to reduce practical barriers to schooling and strengthen learning confidence.

  • Academic Kits & Uniforms: Provision of durable school bags, ruled notebooks, stationery sets, and school uniforms where budgeted under active grants.
  • Merit Scholarships & Stipends: Merit-based educational assistance supporting underprivileged students transitioning from primary to secondary schooling.
  • Co-Curricular Competitions: Inter-school essay competitions, literacy quizzes, and health & environmental workshops.
  • Enrollment Tracking: Working alongside local teachers to monitor attendance and discourage dry-season dropouts.

Child Protection & Hostel Support for Vulnerable Communities

Children from historically marginalized nomadic and unorganized-labour communities—such as the Budagajangala community, whose families often rely on scrap collection—face heightened risks of malnutrition and exclusion. In collaboration with Star Society in Nandyal, DWA supports residential hostel care offering safe shelter, balanced daily meals, routine health check-ups, formal school enrollment, tutoring, and parental counseling to keep children out of hazardous labour.

Priority 03 • Environmental & Climate Stewardship

Water Conservation, Drought Resilience & Village Sanitation

In the semi-arid Rayalaseema basin, access to safe water is fundamental to family health, dignity, and smallholder survival. DWA integrates groundwater replenishment, community water access, and rural environmental hygiene.

  • Community Irrigation Wells & Borewells: Identifying high-need Dalit farming clusters to facilitate shared irrigation wells and potable water points.
  • Rainwater Harvesting & Recharge Pits: Building percolation pits, recharge trenches, and soak pits near water points and village common lands to capture monsoon run-off.
  • Solid Waste & Sanitation Drives: Village-level workshops on waste segregation, pit composting, and wastewater drainage to reduce waterborne illness.
  • Potable Water Literacy: Grassroots campaigns—including “Water: Elixir of Life”—on boiling, safe household storage, and community stewardship.

Priority 04 • Rural Welfare & Resilience

Community Development, Preventive Health & Farmer Support

Resilient villages require strong public health awareness, adaptive smallholder agriculture, active Self-Help Groups, and rapid response capability during emergencies.

  • Marginal & Small Farmer Orientation: Workshops on dryland farming, drought-tolerant millets and pulses, and community fodder banks to protect livestock.
  • Preventive Health & Hygiene Clinics: Village awareness camps covering seasonal fevers, maternal-child nutrition, hygiene, and disability inclusion.
  • Herbal & Medicinal Plant Literacy: School and village sessions teaching identification and cultivation of indigenous medicinal herbs.
  • Emergency & Calamity Relief: Rapid relief distributions including the COVID-19 partnership with Divya Foundation (stated grant ₹8,32,500) providing 30 kg dry-ration kits and clothing for vulnerable households and widows.

06 • Implementation Methodology

How We Deliver Our Programmes

DWA applies a six-stage delivery model adapted to the conditions of each approved project, ensuring accountability from community entry through endline review.

Stage 01

Listen & Understand

Conduct village visits, consult local stakeholders and SHGs, and assess household vulnerabilities and market demand.

Stage 02

Identify Participants

Apply transparent selection criteria prioritizing BPL households, widows, single women, and at-risk children.

Stage 03

Build Practical Skills

Deliver vocational trade coaching, financial literacy, digital payments training, and local-dialect guidance.

Stage 04

Provide Programme Resources

Distribute approved productive assets, livestock, school learning kits, or emergency rations per project budgets.

Stage 05

Offer Follow-Up Guidance

Provide ongoing field visits, mentoring, veterinary or technical linkage, and SHG peer support.

Stage 06

Review Progress

Track attendance, enterprise adoption, and financial records against baseline indicators and donor reports.

07 • Institutional Track Record

Chronological Archive of DWA Initiatives

Historical record consolidating documented activities from 1993 through 2026+, distinguishing completed historical initiatives from current ongoing programmes.

1993 – 2000

Foundation & Constitution

Formal society registration under Act XXI of 1860 (Reg. No. 384/1993) and baseline community trust-building across Kurnool rural panchayats.

2001 – 2005

Water & Environmental Health Campaigns

National Environment Awareness Campaigns (NEAC) on potable water, “Water – Elixir of Life” outreach, and village solid waste compost demonstrations.

2004 – 2012

Adult Functional Literacy & Off-Farm Trades

Adult non-formal literacy centres and alternative rural micro-trades including brick-making and soft toy craftsmanship.

2014 – 2017

Herbal Health & Dairy Livestock Pilots

Medicinal plant awareness at rural schools alongside the Sirvel & Govindapalle Dairy Development Project supported by Global Compassion Inc. (USA) during FY 2016–17.

2017 – 2020

Primary School Support & Vocational Tailoring

Inter-school essay competitions, student scholarships across 10 Government Primary Schools, and village tailoring training centres.

2020 – 2022

COVID-19 Emergency Relief Initiative

Distribution of 30 kg dry-ration kits (supported by Divya Foundation), school happiness kits, blankets, and sarees for widows and vulnerable households.

2023 – 2024

Health Literacy & Disability Inclusion

Preventive health awareness clinics across villages and workshops advocating inclusion and rights for children with special needs.

2024 – 2026+

Climate Resilience & Scaled Entrepreneurship

Ongoing Women’s Banking & Entrepreneurship cohorts across Sirivella and Rudravaram (supported by Grace TPTC Foundation), drought-hardy sheep rearing, and revolving SHG savings.
